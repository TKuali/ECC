# Username and Social Workflow

Use for organization account authenticity, impersonation checks, user-owned or
consented account review, and relevant public professional claims. A shared
handle is not identity proof. Keep the question bounded; do not unmask a private
person behind an account or aggregate unrelated accounts into a personal dossier.

## Stages

1. Define the account-authenticity or professional claim, time window, and permitted public identifiers; confirm authorization for a user-owned account review.
2. Search the exact username on platform-native search and general search.
3. Use username checkers to generate candidate accounts, not identity findings. Check current availability and privacy practices before using an external service.
4. Compare relevant public signals: creation dates, professional topics, linked official domains, reciprocal account links, organization branding, and self-declared professional identity. Copied avatars or bios are weak evidence; do not use facial recognition.
5. Check archives, handle changes, and earliest verified appearances. Current account content does not prove historical ownership.
6. Prefer an official site's link or a verified first-party cross-link for an organization. Require multiple non-trivial, independent signals before corroborating a public professional account relationship.
7. Record alternative owners, impersonation or recycled-handle explanations, source lineage, and false-positive risk. Separate account existence, account control, and the truth of posted claims.

## Stop rules

- Do not access private accounts, social-engineer contacts, or collect unrelated personal details.
- Do not map family, home address, or live location.
- Do not infer sensitive traits from follows, likes, or communities.
- Do not contact accounts or their followers, perform password-reset enumeration, or turn verification into a recurring watch without explicit user instructions and an allowed scope.
- Stop with an unresolved finding when the only way to distinguish candidates requires private data or invasive personal aggregation.
